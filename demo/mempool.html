    Find CPUs

    Every CPU mining rig (XMRig) that is connected to your local network, with the cores and threads it mines on. Click Manage, the dashboard can showcase the stats of one local mining rig's IP at a time.

    CPU rigs can join a fleet too: set them up here, then add their IP addresses under Generate fleet on Find ASIC.

    Find ASIC

    Every ASIC that is connected to your local network. Click Manage, the dashboard can showcase the stats of one local mining rig's IP at a time.

    Generate fleet

    First set up every miner: click Manage, log in, and configure it until it shows Connected. Then paste their IP addresses (also miners in another subnet) and click Generate. You get a fleet dashboard with all of them: each miner's shares fly into the block being mined.

              How to read the blocks

              The blockchain page has two halves, split by the fan: on its left the miners build the next block, on its right the blocks they found are chained together.

              Before the fan

              Inside the miners

              These four steps show how the block that is being hashed right now came to be. Each one is live: it is built from your own node's mempool and chain tip.

              Behind the fan

              The blockchain

              The glow around a mined block tells you who built its block template, the list of transactions inside. The colour of the block itself still shows the fees: the warmer, the more its transactions pay. Every proof-of-work chain has its own rules:

              BitcoinSHA-256 · our own Bitcoin node

              Decentralised (green glow) Decentralised blocks are blocks that are constructed by a decentralised peer-to-peer network of nodes (solominers). These miners construct their own block templates by running their hashrate through their own node and they receive non-custodial payouts directly in the coinbase transaction.Pool architecture: peer-to-peer
              Mempool filters: coming soon

              DATUM/TIDES (blue glow) DATUM/TIDES blocks are blocks that are constructed by mining pools that let miners construct their own block templates with DATUM Gateway. These miners receive non-custodial payouts directly in the coinbase transaction via the TIDES algorithm.Pool architecture: hybrid
              Mempool filters: None

              Stratum V2 (orange glow) Stratum V2 blocks are blocks by miningpools that let miners construct their own block templates with their own node. Miners pointing hashrate to Stratum V2 mining pools receive custodial payouts by third-party pool operators.Pool architecture: centralised
              Mempool filters: None

              Centralised (red glow) Centralised blocks are blocks containing block templates that are constructed by the miningpool. The pool decides which transactions go into a block, which transactions stay out of a block and it pays out participating miners custodial by acting as a third-party service.Pool architecture: centralised
              Mempool filters: None

              Bitcoin BLAKE2bBLAKE2b · our own Bitcoin Knots node

              Decentralised (green glow) Decentralised blocks are blocks that are constructed by a decentralised peer-to-peer network of nodes (solominers). These miners construct their own block templates by running their hashrate through their own node and they receive non-custodial payouts directly in the coinbase transaction.Pool architecture: peer-to-peer
              Mempool filters: coming soon

              DATUM/TIDES (blue glow) DATUM/TIDES blocks are blocks that are constructed by mining pools that let miners construct their own block templates with DATUM Gateway. These miners receive non-custodial payouts directly in the coinbase transaction via the TIDES algorithm.Pool architecture: hybrid
              Mempool filters: rejectparasites/rejecttokens

              Centralised (red glow) Centralised blocks are blocks containing block templates that are constructed by the miningpool. The pool decides which transactions go into a block, which transactions stay out of a block and it pays out participating miners custodial by acting as a third-party service.Pool architecture: centralised
              Mempool filters: None

              MoneroRandomX · our own Monero node

              A Monero block never names its pool, so the glow comes from how the block reward was paid.

              Decentralised (green glow) Decentralised blocks are blocks that are constructed by a decentralised peer-to-peer network of nodes (solominers). These miners construct their own block templates by running their hashrate through their own node and they receive non-custodial payouts directly in the coinbase transaction.Pool architecture: peer-to-peer
              Mempool filters: coming soon

              Decentralised (orange glow) The block was mined on P2Pool. P2Pool is a sharechain: every miner runs their own Monero node and builds their own block, and the reward is paid straight to all the miners inside the coinbase transaction.Pool architecture: peer-to-peer
              Mempool filters: None

              Centralised (red glow) A Monero block that is mined by a centralised pool. The reward is paid to a single address, the pool, which builds the block template and pays its miners as a third-party service.Pool architecture: centralised
              Mempool filters: None

              DogecoinScrypt · our own Dogecoin node

              Decentralised (green glow) Decentralised blocks are blocks that are constructed by a decentralised peer-to-peer network of nodes (solominers). These miners construct their own block templates by running their hashrate through their own node and they receive non-custodial payouts directly in the coinbase transaction.Pool architecture: peer-to-peer
              Mempool filters: coming soon

              Centralised (red glow) Every mined Dogecoin block gets the red glow, whichever pool tag it carries, because Dogecoin pools build the block templates for their miners. No Dogecoin block gets a green or orange glow.Pool architecture: centralised
              Mempool filters: None

              Miner settings

              Everything a miner needs to run, in one place for any brand. Each section says whether it is sent to the miner or only saved here.

              Connect to a miner first: pick one on Find ASIC or Find CPUs, which list every miner powered on in your local network. Settings always apply to the one miner this page is connected to.

                Connect your own node and Electrum server

                Stream a blockchain from your own node. Pick the hash function its chain is mined with, then enter the node, an Electrum server (Electrs, Fulcrum), or both: the node gives full block details and the block your miners work on, Electrum adds the mempool.
